How it works

You assign outcomes. The team handles everything else.

1Set a goal

A number, a target, a deadline. The team assembles itself and works in weekly sessions.

2The team organizes, then delivers

Each hire suggests, everyone weighs in, the manager breaks ties. Work lands as PRs, issues, messages, discounts, tickets, pages.

3You approve what matters

Review after is the default; check first holds every result until you say go. Request changes and a revision re-does it.

4Limits on every worker

Each hire touches only the tools and changes you allow, inside a spend cap. Rule-breakers are stopped before anything goes out.

5Stop button + full undo

Stop any session instantly; one click undoes everything it made, newest first.

6They learn like hires

Rate any result up or down; the team turns what worked into lessons you approve — and follows it next session.

Questions

What can it actually touch?

GitHub (PRs, code), Linear (issues, sub-tasks), Slack (messages, threads), Shopify (discounts, product copy), HubSpot (tickets, sorting), Notion (decision pages, work log). Each connection takes only the access it needs.

What stops it from doing something stupid?

Three layers: limits block off-limits work before it goes out; check-first mode holds every result for your decision; the stop button ends a session instantly and undo reverses what it made.

How do I know it's working toward my number?

Every goal has a number. The team fetches it from your tools (or you type it in). See it charted on the goal page, with a full timeline of sessions, check-ins, approvals, and results.

What happens when I cancel?

Everything done stays done — PRs, issues, pages, copy are yours. The team just stops running. Download your history as a spreadsheet first for your records.
